Depression

What is Depression?

Depression is a common mental health condition that affects how a person feels, thinks, and handles daily activities.

It involves a persistent low mood, sadness, or loss of interest in things that once felt enjoyable.

It is more than a regular bad day or normal mood changes — depression lasts longer and can impact sleep, energy, motivation, and overall well-being.

What the timer is for

  • Tracking how long you’ve gone without self-harm or harmful urges.
  • Measuring the duration of low or difficult moments.
  • Tracking time since your last depressive episode.
  • Recording how long you’ve stayed engaged in grounding or self-care.
  • Building a visible streak of progress toward recovery

Guidance

  • If you feel urges to self-harm (SH)
  • Pause and take a slow breath.
  • Try grounding techniques (holding something cold, noticing 5 things you can see, etc.).
  • Seek a safe distraction like listening to calming music or talking to someone you trust.
  • Remove or step away from anything that could cause harm.

Controlling your environment

  • Stay in a place where you feel safe and not alone, if possible.
  • Reduce overwhelming noise or bright lights.
  • Keep comforting items close (blankets, water, soothing objects).

Getting help

  • Reach out to a trusted person, therapist, or crisis line.
  • If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services.
  • Remember: asking for help is a strength, not a failure.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • “It’s okay to rest. You’re not falling behind — you’re recovering.”
  • “Small steps count. Even the tiniest effort is progress.”
  • “You don’t have to feel okay to deserve care.”
  • “You’re important. Your feelings matter.”
  • “Today doesn’t define you.”

Encouragement

  • “You’ve made it through every tough moment so far — you can make it through this one too.”
  • “You’re not alone. Support exists, and you deserve it.”
  • “It’s okay if all you did today was survive. That’s enough.”
  • “Your worth isn’t determined by your productivity.”

Supportive scripts

  • “Take a slow breath. You’re safe in this moment.”
  • “Talk to yourself kindly — you deserve compassion.”
  • “Try not to judge yourself for what you’re feeling. Feelings are not failures.”

Life-line — reach out

If you’re unsure, call your country’s emergency services (such as 911, 112, or 999).
