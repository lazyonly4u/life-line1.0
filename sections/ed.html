What is an Eating Disorder?

Eating disorders are serious conditions that affect a person’s relationship with food, body image, and emotions.

They involve challenging thoughts and behaviors around eating and can impact physical, mental, and social health.

Common types include anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, ARFID, OSFED, pica, and rumination disorder.

Eating disorders are treatable, and recovery is possible with the right support.

Guidance

Types of eating disorders

  • Anorexia Nervosa: severe restriction of food and fear of weight gain.
  • Bulimia Nervosa: cycles of bingeing and compensatory behaviors.
  • Binge Eating Disorder: recurring episodes of consuming large amounts of food.
  • ARFID: limited intake due to sensory issues or fear of consequences.
  • OSFED: disordered eating that doesn’t fully match other categories.

Controlling your environment

  • Try to maintain a safe and structured routine around meals.
  • Avoid triggering media or conversations about body weight.
  • Surround yourself with supportive people when possible.
  • Keep items that comfort you nearby.

Getting help

  • Eating disorders are serious medical conditions, professional care is important.
  • Reach out to a doctor, therapist, or nutrition specialist.
  • If you feel physically unwell or in danger, seek immediate medical help.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • “Your worth is not defined by your body.”
  • “You deserve nourishment, care, and kindness.”
  • “Recovery is possible, and you deserve it.”
  • “You don’t have to fight this alone.”
  • “Be patient with yourself.”

Encouragement

  • “Every act of self-care is a victory.”
  • “You are more than how you look or what you eat.”
  • “Your body deserves respect, not punishment.”
  • “It’s brave to seek support, and you’re doing great.”

Supportive scripts

  • “Take a breath. Let your body know it’s safe.”
  • “You deserve comfort and compassion, not criticism.”
  • “One step at a time is enough. Healing takes time.”

Eating Disorders

If you are experiencing medical distress, fainting, chest pain, extreme weakness, purging behaviors you cannot stop, or thoughts of self-harm related to eating or body image, this may be a medical emergency.

Please contact emergency services immediately to protect your health and safety.
