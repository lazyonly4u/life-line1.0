Anxiety

What is Anxiety?

Anxiety is a feeling of worry, fear, or tension. It can be triggered by real or imagined threats.

When anxiety rises, the body activates its natural fight, flight, or freeze response, which can cause physical symptoms like a racing heart, shaking, or trouble breathing.

Everyone feels anxious at times, but anxiety becomes a concern when it is strong, frequent, or affects daily life.

What the timer is for

  • Measuring how long a panic attack or anxiety spike lasts.
  • Tracking the time spent doing grounding or breathing exercises.
  • Counting calm time after a stressful moment.
  • Monitoring how long it’s been since your last anxiety episode.
  • Encouraging awareness of progress over time.

Guidance

If you’re having a panic attack

  • Focus on slow, steady breathing (in for 4 seconds, out for 6).
  • Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.
  • Remind yourself: “This will pass. I am safe.”
  • Sit or lie down in a comfortable position.

Types of anxiety

  • Agoraphobia – fear of places where escape feels difficult.
  • Generalized Anxiety Disorder – constant worry about everyday things.
  • Panic Disorder – sudden, intense waves of fear.
  • Social Anxiety – fear of social situations or being judged.
  • Specific Phobias – intense fear of certain objects or situations.
  • Medical/Substance-related anxiety – anxiety caused by health conditions or substances.
  • Selective Mutism, Separation Anxiety, and others – anxiety in specific situations.

Controlling your environment

  • Move to a quieter or familiar space.
  • Dim lights or reduce noise.
  • Keep grounding tools: water, a soft object, calming sounds.

Getting help

  • Talk to a mental-health professional if anxiety affects daily life.
  • Reach out to someone you trust for support.
  • If symptoms become overwhelming or unsafe, seek immediate help.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • “Breathe. This moment will pass.”
  • “Your body is reacting to stress, not danger.”
  • “You don’t need to solve everything right now.”
  • “It’s okay to slow down.”
  • “You are allowed to feel what you’re feeling.”

Encouragement

  • “You’ve gotten through anxious moments before. You can do it again.”
  • “You’re stronger than your panic feels.”
  • “Every small effort to calm yourself is meaningful.”
  • “You’re doing the best you can — and that’s enough.”

Supportive scripts

  • “Notice your surroundings: you are safe right now.”
  • “Let your breath guide you back to the present.”
  • “It’s okay to take breaks, to pause, and to ask for help.”

Anxiety

If your anxiety feels unmanageable, leads to panic attacks, or causes you to feel unsafe or out of control, it’s important to seek immediate assistance.

Emergency services can help stabilize the situation and connect you with appropriate support if your symptoms escalate.
