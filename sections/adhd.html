What is ADHD?

ADHD (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder) is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects attention, motivation, and self-regulation.

People with ADHD may experience difficulties with focus, restlessness, organizing tasks, or controlling impulses.

ADHD often begins in childhood but can continue into adulthood.

With understanding, structure, and support, people with ADHD can learn strategies that help them thrive in daily life.

What the timer is for

  • Tracking how long you’ve been focused on a task
  • Monitoring the time spent on routines or activities
  • Measuring time between distractions
  • Helping with awareness of time passing (time-blindness support)
  • Recording streaks of completed focus sessions

Guidance

Improving inattention

  • Break tasks into small steps.
  • Use timers or reminders to stay on track.
  • Keep your environment organized and minimal.

Improving hyperactivity

  • Let yourself move: stretching, pacing, fidget tools.
  • Take short breaks during tasks.
  • Use calming activities like deep breathing or music.

Improving impulsivity

  • Pause and count to 5 before reacting when you can.
  • Keep a notepad or digital board to jot down thoughts instead of acting immediately.
  • Practice routines that support planning and structure.

Controlling your environment

  • Reduce distractions (noise, clutter, screens).
  • Use visual supports: calendars, checklists, color coding.
  • Create a calm, predictable workspace.

Getting help

  • ADHD professionals can help with diagnosis, strategies, and treatment.
  • Support groups, coaching, or therapy can improve daily functioning.
  • If ADHD is affecting school, work, or relationships, reaching out for help can make a big difference.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • “Your brain works differently, not wrongly.”
  • “It’s okay to take breaks.”
  • “You don’t need to be perfect to be worthy.”
  • “Small actions count, one step is still progress.”
  • “You’re trying, and that matters.”

Encouragement

  • “Your creativity and energy are strengths.”
  • “You can learn new ways to manage things. It’s not too late.”
  • “You’re capable of growth, even when it feels messy.”
  • “Celebrate the little wins. They add up.”

Supportive scripts

  • “Take one small task and start gently.”
  • “You don’t have to do everything at once.”
  • “It’s okay to ask for structure and support, it helps.”
