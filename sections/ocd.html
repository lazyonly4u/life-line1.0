What is OCD?

OCD (Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder) is a mental health condition that causes unwanted, intrusive thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors or mental rituals (compulsions).

People with OCD may feel driven to repeat certain actions to reduce anxiety, even if they know the fears are unlikely to happen.

With the right support and treatment, many people with OCD can learn to manage their symptoms and live fulfilling lives.

What the timer is for

  • Tracking time since completing a compulsion.
  • Tracking time practicing response prevention.
  • Recording how long you've resisted reassurance seeking.
  • Measuring time spent using healthy coping strategies instead of rituals.
  • Building confidence through gradual progress.

Guidance

If you're experiencing intrusive thoughts or the urge to perform compulsions

  • Pause and take a slow breath.
  • Remind yourself: "Thoughts are not facts."
  • Try to delay or reduce the compulsion if it feels safe to do so.
  • Use a grounding technique or focus on a calming activity.

Controlling your environment

  • Keep a calm, comfortable space.
  • Reduce unnecessary stress or distractions.
  • Keep calming items nearby, such as music, water, or a fidget object.
  • Stick to a consistent daily routine when possible.

Getting help

  • OCD is treatable with professional support, including therapy and, for some people, medication.
  • Reach out to a trusted person or mental health professional if symptoms interfere with daily life.
  • If you feel unsafe or are in crisis, contact emergency services immediately.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • "An intrusive thought is not the same as an intention."
  • "You don't have to solve every thought."
  • "Uncertainty is uncomfortable, but it is survivable."
  • "Thoughts aren't facts."
  • "Progress comes from responding differently, not from having perfect thoughts."

Encouragement

  • "You are stronger than your OCD tells you."
  • "Every time you resist a compulsion, you're practicing a new skill."
  • "Recovery isn't about never feeling anxious, it's about learning you can handle the anxiety."
  • "Small victories add up over time."
  • "It's okay if today feels difficult. Progress isn't always linear."

Supportive scripts

  • "I notice this thought, but I don't have to act on it."
  • "I can allow this feeling to exist without responding to it."
  • "Maybe the fear is true, maybe it isn't, and I don't need certainty right now."
  • "My anxiety will rise and fall naturally."
  • "I'm choosing what matters to me instead of what OCD demands."
