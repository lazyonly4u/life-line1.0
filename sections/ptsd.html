PTSD

What is PTSD?

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a mental health condition that can develop after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event.

People with PTSD may experience flashbacks, nightmares, strong anxiety, or intrusive thoughts about what happened.

Although many people feel distress after trauma, PTSD is diagnosed when symptoms last longer than a month and make everyday life difficult.

With support and professional help, people with PTSD can improve and heal.

What the timer is for

  • Tracking the duration of flashbacks, dissociation, or high-stress moments.
  • Counting how long you’ve been grounded or stable.
  • Monitoring how long it has been since your last triggering event.
  • Measuring the time spent doing grounding or self-soothing.
  • Creating a sense of progress by showing increasing stability periods.

Guidance

  • If you’re having flashbacks or intrusive memories
  • Ground yourself: touch a surface, hold something textured, notice your surroundings.
  • Remind yourself: “This is a memory. I am safe right now.”
  • Focus on slow breathing or a calming object.
  • If possible, move to a safe and quiet place.

For nightmares or severe anxiety

  • Turn on a light or orient yourself in the present.
  • Practice a grounding routine or calming activity.
  • Reach out to someone you trust if you need support.

Controlling your environment

  • Keep your space safe, calm, and predictable.
  • Use soft lighting, familiar items, and comforting scents.
  • Avoid sudden noises or overwhelming triggers when possible.

Getting help

  • PTSD is treatable with professional support (therapy, trauma-focused care).
  • If you feel unsafe or overwhelmed, reach out to crisis professionals.
  • Healing takes time — and you don’t have to do it alone.

Gentle reminders & encouragement

Gentle reminders

  • “You’re safe in this moment.”
  • “What you’re feeling is a memory, not the present.”
  • “Your reactions make sense — you went through something hard.”
  • “Healing is not linear, and that’s okay.”
  • “You deserve peace.”

Encouragement

  • “You survived something painful — that makes you incredibly strong.”
  • “It’s okay to reach out and lean on others.”
  • “Every grounding breath is a step forward.”
  • “You’re not broken — you’re healing.”

Supportive scripts

  • “Place your feet on the ground. Feel the present holding you.”
  • “You’re here. You’re safe. You’re in control.”
  • “It’s okay to take your time. You don’t need to rush your healing.”

If you are experiencing severe distress, flashbacks, dissociation, or urges to harm yourself or others due to trauma-related symptoms, please seek emergency help right away.

Immediate support can help ensure your safety and provide grounding and care during intense episodes.
